## Overview
This project was carried out at the **Istituto Italiano di Tecnologia (IIT) - U-VIP Unit**, Genoa, as a central part of my Master's thesis in Biomedical Engineering (Biomedical Instrumentation specialization) at Politecnico di Torino.

The work focused on the validation of a multimodal technological framework designed to assess pediatric visuospatial attention, aiming to overcome the limitations of the traditional paper-based Modified Bells Test. Different devices were utilized, including the TechPAD, a prototype able to create a multisensory environment, and a high-definition eye-tracker, the Tobii Pro Spectrum. A population of typically developing children (aged 4–12 years, SD = 2.3) was analyzed.

## Key Contributions
* **Data Acquisition:** Conducted real-time, on-field acquisition utilizing a Tobii Pro Spectrum eye-tracker and TechPAD, a custom prototype developed by IIT. 
* **Data Processing & ML:** Engineered data post-processing pipelines and performed advanced statistical analysis using MATLAB and Python, integrating machine learning techniques. 
* **Key Outcome:** Provided initial validation of the novel digital adaptation framework proposed in the study.

## Tools & Methods
* **Programming & Analysis:** MATLAB, Python, Machine Learning, Statistical Analyses
* **Hardware & Data Collection:** Tobii Pro Spectrum, Eye-tracking software, TechPAD (Real-time and on-field acquisition)
