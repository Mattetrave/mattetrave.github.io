## Overview

Developed as my Bachelor's thesis project in Biomedical Engineering at Politecnico di Torino, this research was conducted during a 200-hour internship at the Health Management Department of Ospedale Cottolengo in Turin. The project provides a comprehensive comparative analysis of two leading surgical energy modalities—ultrasound (Harmonic®) and bipolar radiofrequency (LigaSure™)—used for tissue dissection and intraoperative hemostasis. 

## Objectives

* **Technical Evaluation:** Analyze the physical and technical principles governing ultrasound and radiofrequency surgical devices, referencing the EU 2017/745 Medical Device Regulations.
* **Clinical Comparison:** Compare outcomes such as lateral thermal damage, operative time, and intraoperative visibility across different surgical applications.
* **Economic Assessment:** Conduct a feasibility analysis utilizing the Health Technology Assessment (HTA) framework and the Diagnosis-Related Group (DRG) reimbursement system.

## Methodology

* **Clinical Literature Review:** Assessed quantitative metrics including thermal spread, smoke generation, and surgical efficiency, confirming that both technologies significantly outperform traditional monopolar electrosurgery.
* **Field Interviews:** Gathered qualitative data through structured consultations with head surgeons in Urology and Breast Surgery to understand practical advantages, such as tissue-specific device preferences and handling during complex procedures.
* **Economic Modeling:** Analyzed hospital expenditure data from 2019 to 2021 to calculate the financial impact of utilizing high-cost, single-use devices relative to standard DRG reimbursements for specific operations (e.g., radical prostatectomy, mastectomy, and bowel resections).

## Key Findings

* **Clinical Efficacy & Safety:** Radiofrequency devices (LigaSure™) generally offer faster operative times, while ultrasound devices (Harmonic®) provide superior thermal control (reduced lateral damage) and maintain clear visual fields by eliminating surgical smoke. 
* **Surgical Preference:** Device selection is highly dependent on the surgical specialty; for instance, bipolar radiofrequency is heavily favored in specific urological enucleations to minimize ischemia risks, whereas devices minimizing lateral thermal spread are prioritized in oncology to preserve healthy margins.
* **Economic Sustainability:** The high unit cost of these single-use devices requires strategic allocation. Their impact on the DRG budget varies significantly, proving highly sustainable for major surgeries (e.g., having a negligible percentage impact on major pelvic surgeries) but economically burdensome for minor procedures.

## Tools & Methods

* **Core Stack:** Health Technology Assessment (HTA), Diagnosis-Related Group (DRG) Cost Analysis, Clinical Literature Review
* **Domain Focus:** Surgical Energy Devices, Healthcare Economics, Medical Device Regulations
